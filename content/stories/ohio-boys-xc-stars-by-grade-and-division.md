*From a freshman breaking 16 minutes to juniors challenging the state's fastest seniors, a look at the top 10 boys in each grade across Ohio's four cross country divisions.*

Brice Fuller has run 14:54.1. Ian Patrick has run 15:02.1. Calvin Watson has run 15:06.3.

Two juniors and a sophomore. Three performances that make it difficult to talk about Ohio's future without talking about its present.

The seniors have plenty to say, too. Versailles' Jackson Spitzer has a 14:54.9 beside his name. Little Miami's Ryne Reynolds has gone 14:57.7. In Division 4, Ansonia's Bennett Lehman leads a senior group that includes Russia's Samuel Phlipot and Sherwood Fairview's Malaki Neilson.

Then there is Lucas Thomas. The Garfield freshman has already broken 16 minutes, giving the youngest class in this feature a headliner of its own.

Looking only at one statewide list can hide some of the most interesting stories. Break the performances down by grade and division, and a different picture emerges: teammates developing together, freshmen arriving with immediate impact, and runners separated by fractions of a second around the top 10 cutoff.

Here is how those groups stack up as September comes to a close.

*About these rankings: This feature uses the fastest reported 5K performances in the Athletic.net and MileSplit lists supplied to Podium Watch, covering results through September 26. Times are displayed to the nearest tenth. These are performance rankings, not championship predictions. Courses, weather, and race conditions differ.*

## Freshmen: The first chapter is moving fast

McAfee is part of a particularly interesting pairing. His **16:24.4** puts him second, while teammate **Dylan Chisanga** sits fifth at **16:36.2**. Two freshmen among the division's first five gives Lakota West a promising pair to watch as this class develops.

The group ends with Mason's **Benjamin Goebel** at **16:57.3**, meaning every runner in this top 10 has already posted a sub-17 performance.

## What to watch next

The youngest runners have already made this more than a senior showcase. Thomas has broken 16 as a freshman. Watson has brought the sophomore standard close to 15. Fuller and Patrick have produced marks that sit near the very top of the entire collection.

There are also promising combinations inside individual programs. Lakota West has two of the first five Division 1 freshmen. Milton-Union has two Division 3 sophomores. Springboro places pairs in both its junior and senior Division 1 lists. Russia has a grade leader in Bixler and additional representation through Smith and Phlipot.

None of those observations decides a championship. These lists do not show every runner on a team, and a season best from one course cannot tell us exactly what will happen on another.

They do give us names to follow. Watch the athletes approaching 16 minutes. Watch the tightly packed groups where a few seconds could move a runner several places. And watch how the runners who have already posted exceptional times respond when the next race asks something different of them.

Ohio has plenty of established talent. Judging by these lists, it also has plenty more arriving.
