# The opening view

Division I begins with a clear leader and a chase that becomes interesting almost immediately. Mason owns the first Podium Watch ranking with 151 points, a total built from two scorers inside the first eight places and enough support behind them to protect the advantage. Springboro is second at 214, while Thomas Worthington is only eight points farther back at 222. That is a healthy opening margin for Mason, but it is not a reason for anyone else to concede August.

The shape of this race matters as much as the order. Springboro and Thomas Worthington both put two runners near the front. Olentangy Berlin, New Albany, Mentor, Dublin Coffman, and Dublin Jerome form a compact second tier where the fifth scorer and the sixth runner can shift several positions. Beavercreek and Westerville North complete a Top 10 in which the difference between ninth and tenth is just three points.

For runners, this is a reminder that a team ranking is created one place at a time. For parents, it is a way to see why five strong performances matter more than one headline time. For coaches, the preseason question is direct: which lineup can turn its fourth, fifth, and sixth positions into fewer points when the field gets crowded?

# How the rankings were created

These rankings use returning runners and recorded 2025 5K performances. Teams were placed in their official 2026 OHSAA divisions. The twenty five fastest eligible teams by five runner average formed each simulated field, with up to seven returners entered per team.

Five runners scored. Sixth and seventh runners displaced opponents and decided ties. The lowest score won, with ties settled by the sixth runner and then the seventh. The number beside a team is its simulated cross country score, not its total time or five runner average.

The recorded performances came from different courses, meets, weather conditions, and points in the 2025 season. No course adjustment was applied. The result measures returning strength through one consistent scoring model, but it does not guarantee what will happen when teams race in 2026.
