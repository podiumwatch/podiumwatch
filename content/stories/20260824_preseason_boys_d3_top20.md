# The opening view

Ten points. That is the entire preseason difference between Woodridge and Bryan in Division III. Woodridge scores 123, Bryan scores 133, and both teams arrive with five runner structures that can hold up when the field begins to compress. Fairfield Union is third at 164, bringing the strongest first scorer among the top three but a more demanding fifth runner assignment. Chagrin Falls follows at 195 and keeps four teams inside 72 points of the lead.

The next layer is even tighter. CF Northwest and Minerva both score 242, with CF Northwest taking fifth on the sixth runner tiebreak. Versailles is only one point behind them at 243. West Liberty-Salem sits eighth at 269. Those totals create a division with a close favorite race, a separate three team duel separated by one point, and several lineups that can move quickly if the fourth or fifth scorer improves.

Division III is not asking one question. It is asking whether Woodridge can protect a ten point lead, whether Bryan's compact five can become even more efficient, and whether the front running power of Fairfield Union, Versailles, or another challenger can pull the rest of a lineup upward.

# How the rankings were created

These rankings use returning runners and recorded 2025 5K performances. Teams were placed in their official 2026 OHSAA divisions. The twenty five fastest eligible teams by five runner average formed each simulated field, with up to seven returners entered per team.

Five runners scored. Sixth and seventh runners displaced opponents and decided ties. The lowest score won, with ties settled by the sixth runner and then the seventh. The number beside a team is its simulated cross country score, not its total time or five runner average.

The recorded performances came from different courses, meets, weather conditions, and points in the 2025 season. No course adjustment was applied. The result measures returning strength through one consistent scoring model, but it does not guarantee what will happen when teams race in 2026.
