A junior from Napoleon has the fastest mark in the field. A sophomore from Aurora is next. And the quickest freshman in these rankings comes from Bellaire.

Ohio's girls cross country talent reaches across school sizes and graduating classes. As September closes, Gwen Huddle's 17:26.4, Audrey Miller's 17:42.8 and Paisley Farrier's 18:23.7 offer three different reasons to pay attention. Behind them are crowded rankings, promising groups of classmates and athletes separated by fractions of a second.

Podium Watch's comparison brings together 160 runners across 16 lists, with a top 10 for each grade in Divisions 1 through 4. Ten of the featured athletes have reported performances under 18 minutes. Some groups have a clear leader on time. Others have several runners packed into a few seconds.

These are performance lists rather than predictions of championship finishes. Courses, weather and race conditions vary, and the next race will give every runner a new opportunity to change the picture.

*About the rankings: This feature combines the supplied Athletic.net and MileSplit girls 5K lists through September 29, 2026, retaining the faster reported performance and displaying times to one decimal place. It represents the results provided for this comparison, not an independently exhaustive statewide database.*

## Freshmen

### Division 1: Hattie Kane leads a crowded freshman field

Thomas Worthington's Hattie Kane owns the quickest Division 1 freshman mark in this comparison, an 18:46.6 from the Neal Charske Fairmont Firebird Invitational. ## What to watch as October begins

Huddle's leading time is an obvious place to start, but the wider story has several threads. Division 2's sophomore class puts three runners under 18 minutes and all ten under 19. Beavercreek supplies three Division 1 freshmen. Watterson puts three juniors into its division's top 10. Both Division 1 and Division 2 have entire senior top tens under 19 minutes.

The freshmen offer another compelling comparison. Farrier, Maschek, Skeen-Adrine and Kane lead their respective divisions, with only 22.9 seconds separating the four marks. Each has already given her program a reason to be excited about this class.

October will bring new courses, new competition and new versions of these lists. For the runners featured here, September has already provided a performance worth recognizing. The next chapter is theirs to run.
