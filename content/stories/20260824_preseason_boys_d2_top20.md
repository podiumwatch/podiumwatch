# The opening view

CVCA arrives at the top of the Division II preseason board with 181 points and a lineup that makes sense in a championship style field. Its first three scorers are firmly placed, its fourth and fifth keep the total controlled, and its sixth runner lands only two places behind the fifth. That is the kind of structure coaches trust because it offers more than one way to survive a difficult race.

Lexington follows at 229, and its story is impossible to miss: four scorers are inside the first 30 places before the fifth arrives at 143. Massillon Perry is five points behind Lexington at 234, but it gets there with a much tighter five runner spread. Hoover and Bowling Green are separated by a single point for fourth and fifth. Hudson and Granville are also one point apart. Even before the season begins, the standings show multiple races inside the race.

This division rewards teams that can finish the lineup. A fast first runner matters, but the biggest preseason opportunities belong to teams that already have three or four strong pieces and can bring the final scorer closer to them.

# How the rankings were created

These rankings use returning runners and recorded 2025 5K performances. Teams were placed in their official 2026 OHSAA divisions. The twenty five fastest eligible teams by five runner average formed each simulated field, with up to seven returners entered per team.

Five runners scored. Sixth and seventh runners displaced opponents and decided ties. The lowest score won, with ties settled by the sixth runner and then the seventh. The number beside a team is its simulated cross country score, not its total time or five runner average.

The recorded performances came from different courses, meets, weather conditions, and points in the 2025 season. No course adjustment was applied. The result measures returning strength through one consistent scoring model, but it does not guarantee what will happen when teams race in 2026.

# What could change

Summer development, health, race execution, and normal improvement can change this order. One runner moving through a crowded pack can lower a team score while also displacing rival scorers.

The current lineups show several specific opportunities: Lexington currently has a 130.74 second spread and a fifth score of 143rd, Big Walnut currently has a 23.93 second spread and a fifth score of 77th, and Loveland currently has a 109.80 second spread and a fifth score of 121st. A tighter fifth position or a stronger sixth runner could produce an immediate reward. The raw times still require context because courses, weather, meets, and timing within the season differed. No course adjustment was applied.
