# The opening view

Milford begins the Division I season with the number one ranking and a scoring total that explains why. Milford posts 109 points behind the first finisher in the simulated field, then adds four more scorers inside the first 46 places. Mason is second at 154. Massillon Jackson is third at 173. Both challengers have clear strengths, but Milford starts with fewer exposed places across the full scoring five.

The middle of the Top 10 is almost a separate meet. Sycamore scores 190, Oak Hills 196, and Gahanna Lincoln 197. Six points cover fourth through sixth. Olentangy Liberty and Lancaster later enter another close exchange, while several teams outside the Top 10 have front runners capable of changing a race immediately.

For athletes, the rankings show how one teammate can lift the whole group. For families, they explain why a team with the fastest average is not automatically the team with the lowest race score. For coaches, the key numbers are the fifth scorer and the sixth runner, especially when 25 complete teams place as many as seven athletes into the same simulated field.

# How the rankings were created

These rankings use returning runners and recorded 2025 5K performances. Teams were placed in their official 2026 OHSAA divisions. The twenty five fastest eligible teams by five runner average formed each simulated field, with up to seven returners entered per team.

Five runners scored. Sixth and seventh runners displaced opponents and decided ties. The lowest score won, with ties settled by the sixth runner and then the seventh. The number beside a team is its simulated cross country score, not its total time or five runner average.

The recorded performances came from different courses, meets, weather conditions, and points in the 2025 season. No course adjustment was applied. The result measures returning strength through one consistent scoring model, but it does not guarantee what will happen when teams race in 2026.
