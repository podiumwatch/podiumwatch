2026 Ohio cross country preview

Division 3 Boys Top 25 Returning Runners

Podium Watch ranks the top 25 returning Division 3 boys for the upcoming Ohio high school cross country season, using 2025 cross country results, 2026 track performances, championship experience, and projected development.

Published July 28, 2026, by Podium Watch

A deep division with several legitimate contenders

Division 3 enters the 2026 season with an unusual mix of established state performers, major track breakouts, talented young runners, and athletes arriving through the new OHSAA division assignments. The top of the ranking includes two runners who have already broken 15 minutes for 5K, while the rest of the top 10 includes state placers and athletes who ran close to nine minutes in the 3200.

These are preseason rankings, not a prediction of the exact state championship finish. Cross country courses, weather, race tactics, and team goals can all affect a time. The purpose is to compare the strongest available evidence and identify the returning runners who enter the season with the best overall cases.

Ranking method: State championship cross country performance receives the most weight. Other verified 5K results come next, followed by 2026 track marks in the 1600 and 3200. The list uses the OHSAA divisions for the 2026 season, so several runners competed in a different division during 2025.

Number 1

Brice Fuller

2025 5K14:49.06
2026 track8:56.36 3200, 4:12.69 1600

Why he is ranked here

Fuller begins at number one because he owns the strongest collection of marks in the division. He ran 14:49.06 at the NXR Midwest Regional, then produced 8:56.36 in the 3200 and 4:12.69 in the 1600. No other returning Division 3 runner matches that combination of cross country performance, track strength, and remaining development time.

What could change this ranking

The largest early season questions surround track standouts who are ready to lower their 5K bests, young runners who already produced advanced cross country times, and athletes entering Division 3 through realignment. Head to head invitationals will matter more than isolated times because they allow the leading runners to compete on the same course under the same conditions.

Fuller begins at number one, but Nathaniel Gockerman, Smelcer, Bendo, Spitzer, Perdue, Zang, Cole, Novakovic, Runkle, and Wyatt Gockerman all have evidence that supports a place near the front. Division 3 should have one of the deepest individual races in Ohio when the postseason arrives.
