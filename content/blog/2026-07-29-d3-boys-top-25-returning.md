Podium Watch ranks the top 25 returning Division 3 boys for the upcoming Ohio high school cross country season.

Division 3 enters 2026 with an unusual mix of established state performers, major track breakouts, talented young runners, and athletes entering the division through the new OHSAA assignments. The top of the ranking includes two runners who have already broken 15 minutes for 5K, while the rest of the top 10 includes state placers and athletes who ran close to nine minutes in the 3200.

These are preseason rankings, not a prediction of the exact state championship finish. Cross country courses, weather, race tactics, and team goals can all affect a time. The purpose is to compare the strongest available evidence and identify the returning runners who enter the season with the best overall cases.

## How the rankings were built

State championship cross country performance receives the most weight. Other verified 5K results come next, followed by 2026 track marks in the 1600 and 3200.

The list uses the OHSAA divisions for the 2026 season, so several runners competed in a different division during 2025.

## 25. Vincent Campbell

**School:** Meadowbrook

**Class:** Sophomore

**2025 5K:** 16:32.14

**2026 track:** 9:25.92 3200, 4:22.57 1600

### Why he is ranked here

Campbell earns the final position because his spring track season showed ability far beyond his current cross country best. A 9:25.92 in the 3200 as a freshman is one of the strongest long distance track marks anywhere in the lower half of this list. The main question is not whether Campbell has the talent, it is how quickly that talent appears over 5,000 meters. He has one of the clearest breakout paths in Division 3.

## What could change this ranking

The largest early season questions surround track standouts who are ready to lower their 5K bests, young runners who already produced advanced cross country times, and athletes entering Division 3 through realignment.

Head to head invitationals will matter more than isolated times because they allow the leading runners to compete on the same course under the same conditions.

Brice Fuller begins at number one, but Nathaniel Gockerman, Joel Smelcer, Connor Bendo, Jackson Spitzer, Landen Perdue, Cole Zang, Asher Cole, Luca Novakovic, Connor Runkle, and Wyatt Gockerman all have evidence that supports a place near the front.

Division 3 should have one of the deepest individual races in Ohio when the postseason arrives.
